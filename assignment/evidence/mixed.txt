Mixed rollout experiment (s1=tunnel.p4, s2=tunnel_v1.p4)

Prediction written in REPORT.md before sending:
s2 would extract only proto_id and dst_id, start IPv4 parsing one byte early
at hop_limit, but still use its valid old tunnel header and tunnel table to
forward. I predicted that h2 would receive a malformed one-byte-shifted
packet, and that the old switch would not consume the new hop limit.

Delivery packet sent from h1:
  python3 send.py 10.77.0.2 mixed-delivery --tunnel 2 --hops 4
The sender's tunnel bytes were:
  12 12 00 02 04
The packet did not produce a new decoded line in logs/h2.txt. An unfiltered
capture at h2 showed that bytes did arrive, but they were malformed:

  13:33:09.505425 00:00:00:00:00:01 > ff:ff:ff:ff:ff:ff,
  ethertype Unknown (0x1212), length 60
  0000: ffff ffff ffff 0000 0000 0001 1212
  0010: 0002 0345 0000 2900 0100 0040 1066 270a
  0020: 4d00 010a 4d00 0210 e110 e100 15d1 206d
  0030: 6978 6564 2d63 6170 7475 7265

The important bytes are 12 12 00 02 03 45 ...: old s2 emitted its
four-byte tunnel header (12 12 00 02), then emitted an IPv4 header whose
first byte was the new hop byte 03. The new h2 decoder consumes that 03 as
hop_limit and starts IPv4 at 45, but the old deparser emitted only 03 plus
the first 19 bytes of the original IPv4 header. The IP header is therefore
short by one byte and its payload boundaries are wrong, so the UDP/IP packet
is no longer decodable. This is why delivery failed even though s2 forwarded
to port 2. logs/s2.txt records Bytes parsed: 18 and 38, then dst_id=0002 and
egress port 2.

Loop test:
I changed s2's dst_id=2 entry to port 1, then sent
  python3 send.py 10.77.0.2 mixed-loop --tunnel 2 --hops 4
No packet reached h2. logs/s2.txt shows the old switch repeatedly parsed
18/38 bytes and forwarded to port 1. logs/s1.txt shows the new switch
parsed 19/39 bytes and forwarded back to s2 for five processing events;
on the sixth event its parsed hop limit was zero, so it selected egress 511
and dropped the packet. The new hop limit therefore still stopped this
particular loop, but only because the new s1 was traversed repeatedly;
the old s2 neither parsed nor decremented that byte.

After the test, I restored s2's dst_id=2 entry to port 2.
